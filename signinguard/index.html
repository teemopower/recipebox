Sign-in Guard

Know what an app wants before you click Allow.

When a site asks you to “Sign in with Google” or Microsoft, it can also ask to read your email, open your files or send mail as you. Scammers use this to take over accounts without ever needing your password. Sign-in Guard explains every request in plain words, right on the permission screen.

  • Low risk: the app only learns who you are.
  • Check first: it wants some of your data, like your calendar or contacts.
  • High risk: it wants your email, all your files, or control of your account.

Plain-word verdicts

Every permission becomes a sentence you can understand, riskiest first, with the site you’ll be sent back to.

Fake window warnings

Some scam pages draw a fake sign-in box inside the page. Sign-in Guard spots common ones and tells you to leave.

Private by design

Runs only in your browser. It never sees your password and sends nothing anywhere.

Already clicked Allow somewhere?

Review and remove apps that can reach your account: Google Account · Microsoft account · work or school Microsoft account.

The honest part

Sign-in Guard reads the permission list a site sends to Google or Microsoft. It can’t see what an app does with access afterwards, and its fake-window check catches common tricks, not every one. It’s a second opinion, not antivirus.
